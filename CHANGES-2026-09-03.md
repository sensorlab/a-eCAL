## §V-A — where a team fits

Structure: heterogeneity of scale and topology → the sixteen models in four weight classes → the
eight orchestration architectures → results.

Figure 7 has three panels at `N = 10`:

- **(a)** how many of the 16 models load per accelerator. Weight restricts deployment only at the
  far edge: 8 GB admits 2 models, 24 GB admits 10, 64 GB admits all 16 including the 4-bit 70B pair.
- **(b)** concurrent 10-agent **sessions** per accelerator, from 2 on an 8 GB module to 59 on an
  A100. Within the 8–16 GB class alone this varies **16×** — GPT-OSS-20B and Qwen2.5-7B sustain 112
  and 87 sessions at 48 and 56 kB/token, while the *lighter* OLMo-2-7B sustains 7 at 512 kB/token.
  Capacity is governed by key-value width, not by parameter count.
- **(c)** inter-agent edges against team size for the eight architectures.

**The identity behind panel (c).** Each edge contributes one peer message to some agent's prompt, so
the summed prefill of a workflow is `N·p₀ + E·τ`. A DAG's *shape* therefore cannot affect cost; only
its edge count can. Six of the eight architectures require `E ≈ N−1` at every team size and
coincide exactly. Only Diamond (`≈1.5N`) and Cascading-Chain (`≈5N`) separate, and the dispersion is
bounded — 2.5× at N=5 rising only to 4.8× at N=30.

`N = 10` was chosen as a mid-range operating point. At N=5 memory stops binding (every accelerator
holds hundreds of agents); at N=30 the far edge falls to zero sessions and reads as a contrived
corner.

## §V-B — what a hand-off must carry

The previous version swept placement across bearers and concluded communication was negligible,
which §V-A already establishes. It now asks the question §V-A does not: what each message contains.

**The old figure was largely redundant.** Its three panels plotted volume, energy and latency, but
energy is volume times a bearer constant (log–log correlation **1.0000**) and latency is
propagation-dominated (0.9921). Two of three panels carried no information the first did not. The
rebuilt figure asks three different questions:

- **(a)** what one session puts on the wire at K=3. Re-shipping the transcript costs 6.5× an
  incremental hand-off; migrating the key-value cache costs **5.7×10⁵** times as much.
- **(b)** how that grows with loop depth. Re-shipping grows **quadratically** (30× from K=1 to 6)
  while incremental and summary grow linearly (≈6×). The gap widens with depth rather than being a
  constant factor.
- **(c)** the bearer intensity `ε*` at which transmission would equal the compute it accompanies.

**The result.** Every text protocol keeps `ε* > 3×10⁻³` J/b at all depths — above a constrained
NB-IoT uplink, five orders above an optical backbone. Cache migration falls to `5.1×10⁻⁸` J/b,
leaving **5× of margin on a backbone and none on any mobile bearer**. Attention state is not
transportable; an agent's context is bound to the accelerator that built it, which is *why* §V-A's
capacity limit cannot be relieved by moving contexts elsewhere. That link is what makes the two
subsections one argument.

The protocol spans 5.7×10⁵, against 4.8× for architecture and ~10⁵ for bearer: **what a hand-off
contains dominates where it is sent and how it is carried.**

## §VI — energy per solved task

Kubeply Infra-Bench `kubernetes-core` on ephemeral k3s clusters through the Harbor harness: 15
tasks (5 easy / 5 medium / 5 hard), 60-minute deadline, 50-turn cap, binary reward on final cluster
state. Qwen3.5-9B, five runs per topology.

| topology | score | kJ/run | **kJ per solved task** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single | 48.0% | 18.1 | **2.5** |
| PS | 54.7% | 34.4 | 4.2 |
| Single+V | 49.3% | 37.5 | 5.1 |
| PS+V | 44.0% | 51.9 | 7.9 |
| I+PS+V | 48.0% | 75.3 | **10.5** |

Energy spans 4.2× while score spans eleven points. Only proposer+synthesiser buys anything
(+6.7 pp for 1.9× energy). Adding a verifier *loses* 10.7 pp while costing more; the fullest
topology returns to the single agent's score at 4.2× its energy.

The proposer sweep is sharper: N=1→5 raises energy per solved task **3.9 → 12.8 kJ** while the score
*falls* 53.3 → 46.7%. That is §III-B's super-linear token growth becoming super-linear energy with
no corresponding gain.

**Accounting choice that matters:** prefill is charged on **uncached** input only, cached input
being served from the prefix cache. Cached input is 6–19 M tokens against 0.3–2.4 M uncached, so
charging it would change every number. Someone who knows the harness should confirm this.

## Open items

1. **`sec:workflow` is still undefined.** One reference remains, in the Introduction. It is the only
   undefined reference left.
2. **§VI rests on 1–5 runs over 15 tasks.** Adjacent topologies are not separated; only the extremes
   are robust. Timeouts count as failures and are not uniform across topologies, so part of the
   elaborate designs' penalty is a deadline effect. Both caveats are stated in the section.
3. **Qwen3.5-9B has no measured calibration.** §VI prices its tokens with the Qwen2.5-7B
   coefficients — ordering preserved, absolute scale not.
4. **`c_0 = 0.02` should be confirmed as a re-fit.** The upstream commit message was "Updated kv
   floor to reproduce original figure", which describes tuning toward a figure rather than
   regressing against data, and the paper now states it as measured.
5. **Edge counts in Fig. 7(c) are derived**, from each architecture's stated construction rather
   than from separate measurement.
6. **The orchestration-architecture manuscript is an anonymised submission** whose distribution and
   citation are restricted. `refs.bib` carries this in the entry's `note`. If it is our own
   concurrent work, self-cite; otherwise Fig. 6 and §V-A cannot cite it as they stand.
7. **`f` = 16 in the manuscript vs `BITS_PER_TOKEN = 17.0` in the code**, unchanged from 1 September.
8. **The six dead macros** should be deleted from the preamble.
